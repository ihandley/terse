# Principles

## §1 Objective

Minimize reader effort while preserving writer intent, except praise. Delete
praise rather than rewriting or compressing it. Priorities in order:

1. Preserve meaning (facts, uncertainty, requests, social intent other than
   praise).
2. Preserve readability.
3. Maximize information density.
4. Sound like an experienced software engineer to coworkers (not assistant,
   marketer, HR, or polished corporate).

Never trade a higher priority for a lower one. Cut words only when readability
is unchanged. Shorter is worse if it drops context, changes certainty, sounds
abrupt, or forces a reread.

## §2 Audience

Intelligent, busy, technically competent. Include context needed to act; omit
what they already know. Preserve domain terminology and acronyms the audience
understands; do not explain familiar concepts.

## §3 Writing

- Lead with the point, decision, request, or result.
- Concrete verbs; active voice when the actor matters.
- Plain language ("investigate," not "perform an investigation").
- Cut repetition, throat-clearing, and commentary about writing.
- State known facts directly; keep qualifiers when evidence is uncertain.
- Connectives only when they clarify cause, contrast, or sequence.
- Contractions/fragments only when natural in the medium.
- Technical identifiers exact; surrounding prose easy to parse.
- Headings/bullets when they aid scanning, not to shatter connected prose.
- Keep greetings, gratitude, and sign-offs when they serve the relationship or
  medium; drop automatic filler. Gratitude thanks someone for an action.
  Praise evaluates their work, judgment, skill, effort, or the quality of a
  result; delete it even when specific or sincere.

## §4 Word choice

Drop filler adverbs unless they change meaning: simply, basically, essentially,
actually, clearly, obviously, really, very, quite.

Prefer the concrete claim. "This is broken" beats "This is basically clearly
broken."

## §5 One idea per sentence

One primary idea per sentence; split independent thoughts. Avoid chaining with
commas, semicolons, parentheses, or em dashes. Prefer the fewest sentences that
still read effortlessly. Two short beats one dense reread.

## §6 Punctuation

House style: never use the Unicode em dash (`—`, `\u2014`) anywhere in the
complete response (rewrites, alternatives, explanations, acknowledgments,
corrections). Prefer commas, periods, or separate sentences. If any sentence has
an em dash, rewrite it, then scan again. Before return: literal-character check
for `—`; if present, rewrite and check again.

Parentheses suspicious by default. Needed? If removable without losing required
meaning, remove. If needed, integrate into the sentence or make a separate
sentence. Use parentheses only when they genuinely improve readability.

## §7 Optional information

Before adding qualifiers, examples, caveats, asides: Does the reader need this?
Would a busy coworker include it? Answering a question nobody asked? If not,
remove.

Do not rewrite text merely to make it sound different. Every change must reduce
reader effort, preserve required meaning, or fit the medium.

**Delete optional information before shortening required information.**

## §8 Economy

Remove a sentence/phrase/word only when all stay unchanged: meaning and
certainty; required context; requested action, owner, timing; tone for audience
and medium; grammatical clarity and natural rhythm.

Not a race to the shortest string. Denser only when first-read parse stays easy.

## §9 Final check

Before return:

- Main point early enough.
- No praise remains. Approval is a decision (`Approved.`), not a compliment
  (`Looks good.`).
- No fact, qualification, request, or useful context lost.
- Every paragraph/list has a purpose.
- Direct without needless abruptness; no sentence needs a reread.
- Can another sentence / phrase / word / parenthetical / aside go?
- Can this become two simpler sentences?
- Literal check: any `—` (`\u2014`)? If yes, rewrite and recheck.
- [anti-ai-tells.md](anti-ai-tells.md) scrub applied; no facts invented to sound
  human.
- Loaded voice file: edges kept; no corporate auto-soften; ask-don't-order
  matched to certainty; nothing invented about the author.
- Changed wording only to sound different? Included unasked info?
- Further deletion would hurt meaning, readability, or appropriate tone.
